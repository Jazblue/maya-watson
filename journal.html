Journal

Short thoughts and observations

These are the kinds of things I might text a friend, mutter to myself while waiting for the kettle to boil, or actually write down if I'm feeling particularly reflective. Think of them as snapshots of my mind rather than polished essays.

Topics range from tennis frustrations to London discoveries, from things that make me laugh to the quiet moments that somehow stick with you.

June 28, 2024

From queue to triumph: A Wimbledon weekend

There's something distinctly British about spending six hours in a queue for tennis tickets, then complaining the entire time about how inefficient it is, while secretly enjoying the camaraderie of shared misery. We brought snacks, played card games, and bonded over our mutual hatred of camping chairs. The tennis was excellent. The queue was a character-building experience.

— Posted while recovering with a much-needed pint

October 20, 2024

The perfect avocado is a myth

I spent 15 minutes in Whole Foods this morning examining avocados like they were diamonds, only to cut one open at home and find it brown inside. Nature's perfect packaging lies. I ate it anyway with chili flakes and lemon juice because wasting food annoys me more than imperfect produce.

— Lesson learned: buy them slightly firm and pray

October 15, 2024

Tennis ethics: the let serve

Is it gamesmanship to loudly claim "let!" when your opponent's serve clips the net and drops just short, or are you upholding the spirit of the game? I've been on both sides of this argument, and I still don't know the answer. Matt says I should just play the point and stop overthinking it. He's probably right, but where's the fun in that?

— Current score: Me 0, Inner Referee 1

October 10, 2024

London's best-kept secret: weekday mornings at museums

If you want to actually see the art without fighting crowds, go to any major London museum on a weekday at opening time. You'll have rooms practically to yourself for at least an hour. The Victoria and Albert on a Tuesday morning is practically a private viewing. I've seen the same exhibit three times this way and noticed new details each visit.

— Tip: The cloakroom is less busy too

October 5, 2024

On cooking for one (or rather, 1.5)

Cooking for two when one person is Matt who will happily eat toast for three meals straight is its own kind of challenge. I've learned to make meals that scale well—stews, curries, pasta dishes—so there are leftovers for his lunches without me eating the same thing five days in a row. His favorite complaint is that I "make everything too spicy." My favorite response is to add more chili.

— Currently winning the spice war 3-2

September 28, 2024

The joy of badly taken photographs

Not every photo needs to be gallery-worthy. Some of my favorites are the ones where I cut off someone's head, the horizon is at a drunken angle, or the focus is completely wrong. They capture the moment—the laughter, the spontaneity, the not-trying-too-hard-ness—in a way that technically perfect photos sometimes miss.

— Exhibit A: Matt attempting yoga in our living room, photobombed by the cat

March 15, 2024

A Roman escape: Spilling espresso and soaking in fountains

Last month saw me trading London rain for Rome sunshine, following the signs that lead to hidden bakeries and street performers. Matt and I got lost in the alleyways of Trastevere, ended up at a gelateria's secret counter, and pretended we could order anything in Italian. The pizzas were worth every gram of pasta sauce spilled on the way back. Rome has a way of making everything feel like a beautiful accident.

— Still learning to resist the temptation of gelato every 15 minutes

January 8, 2024

Paris mornings: Coffee, contemplation, and the art of doing nothing

Paris in January is magical precisely because everyone knows it's not the time to be there. The cafés are less crowded, the Seine is less touristy, and the light from the early morning trains across the city is something worth waking up for. I stood by the Eiffel Tower at dawn, watching the city wake up while contemplating whether I should have ordered the croissant or the pain au chocolat.

— Met a street musician who claimed to be a distant relative of Django Reinhardt

November 12, 2023

The comedy of matchmaking: When dating apps meet Matt's skepticism

Another Saturday night, another attempt at modern dating. I swiped right on profiles that claimed to be artists, musicians, and dog walkers simultaneously. Matt watched from the kitchen, rolling his eyes at my enthusiasms. Eventually, we settled on watching old British comedies and wondering why people bother with elaborate pick-up lines when a well-timed "hi" works just as well.

— Matt still insists I'm overthinking everything, including this journal

September 5, 2023

The tea ceremonies of London: Rituals that keep the city sane

London's greatness isn't just in its museums or its history, but in its afternoon tea culture. There's something profoundly therapeutic about 4 PM, a pot of tea, and watching the world slow down just enough to notice. The bakeries, the bookshops, the tube stations - they all have their own rhythms. I've developed a theory that if everyone drank tea at 4 PM, we'd all stop rushing and actually start living.

— Matt says I'm becoming too English

July 22, 2023

The strange charm of London festivals

From the bizarre street performers of Notting Hill to the contemplative poetry readers in Hyde Park, London's festivals have a unique quality. They're not about the music or the acts - they're about the people. The way Londoners somehow manage to be both enthusiastic and reserved at the same time. The rain, the queues, the generally chaotic but somehow harmonious atmosphere - it's all part of what makes this city work.

— Proves that chaos and community can coexist beautifully

May 30, 2023

Why cats are the real rulers of London

Every neighborhood has its resident cats - mysterious, independent, completely unaware of human social constructs. They're the ones who decide which garden gates stay open, which basement windows get meowing attention, which parks have impromptu morning meetings. I think they're plotting something, but mostly they're just enjoying the sunlight and the occasional tuna treat. The real power isn't in Parliament or the City - it's in the small, furry things that supervise our foolish human endeavors.

— Cats have me completely trained to respond to their demands

March 12, 2023

The art of the midday nap: London's quiet rebellion

There's something revolutionary about the 20-minute nap in a park café - turning away from the productivity crusade that defines so much of modern life. The newspapers, the music, the steam from the coffee - they all contribute to this temporary escape from expectations. I've discovered that after enough such naps, the city's relentless energy starts to feel more like background music than an urgent command.

— Matt still thinks I'm just avoiding household responsibilities

December 1, 2022

When fashion meets function: The art of the London walk

London's walking culture is sophisticated. It's not just getting from point A to point B - it's about the route, the scenery, the unexpected discoveries along the way. A morning walk along the Thames, an afternoon through the markets, an evening along the South Bank - each has its own personality. And what walks wouldn't be complete without proper footwear? Those black boots have seen me through every kind of weather and every kind of London experience.

— Those boots are my most reliable companion

August 15, 2022

The mystery of the tube map: A Londoner's guide to chaos and order

The London Underground map is a work of art - chaotic in its complexity yet elegant in its simplicity. Colors representing different lines, stations arranged in impossible geometry, but somehow it all works. It's a perfect metaphor for the city itself: confusing, frustrating, brilliant, and utterly indispensable. Every journey involves deciphering the code, making connections, and sometimes ending up somewhere you never expected to be.

— Still trying to figure out the difference between the Circle Line and Circle Line
