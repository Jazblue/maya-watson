Journal

Short thoughts and observations

These are the kinds of things I might text a friend, mutter to myself while waiting for the kettle to boil, or actually write down if I'm feeling particularly reflective. Think of them as snapshots of my mind rather than polished essays.

Topics range from tennis frustrations to London discoveries, from things that make me laugh to the quiet moments that somehow stick with you.

October 04, 2026

The Hidden Courtyard Behind Borough Market

I stumbled upon a secret today while navigating the lunchtime chaos at Borough Market – a tucked-away courtyard behind the cheese stall that feels like a whole other world. Matt and I were dodging tourists and sampling oysters when he pointed out a narrow archway I'd walked past a dozen times. Beyond it: strings of fairy lights, mismatched furniture, and the kind of quiet that only exists when you know where to look.

We grabbed two glasses of sparkling elderflower from a hidden bar and claimed a corner table, watching the market frenzy through the arched doorway like we'd discovered a private window into the city's heartbeat. Matt laughed when I insisted we come back tomorrow with our camera, saying I treat every London backstreet like a potential photoshoot. He's not wrong – there's something thrilling about finding these pockets of calm amid the urban rush.

It reminded me that London's magic isn't just in the grand sights, but in the accidental discoveries that make you feel like you've been let in on a local secret. Even after all these years, the city still manages to surprise you if you're willing to wander off the beaten path.

— Best discovery since we found that 24-hour pie shop near Waterloo.

June 28, 2024

The Wimbledon Queue: Six Hours of Shared Misery and Sudden Joy

There's something gloriously absurd about queuing for Wimbledon tickets at 5 AM with a thermos of tea that's gone lukewarm, a deck of cards, and Matt's endless commentary on the sheer unfairness of British queuing culture. We spent six hours inching forward, complaining about everything and nothing, playing ridiculous games of gin rummy, and somehow - against all odds - ending up with Centre Court tickets for the women's final.

The match itself was a revelation: Serena's serve like thunder, the crowd's roar like a physical thing, and Matt jumping up and spilling his beer when she broke serve in the second set. We laughed until we cried, then ate strawberries and cream with our fingers because why bother with manners when you're this happy?

— Still wearing the sunburn as a badge of honor

May 15, 2024

Roman Nights: When Gelato Becomes a Love Language

Rome in May is a symphony of sensations - the scent of jasmine clinging to ancient walls, the sound of Vespa tires on cobblestones, and the way the light hits the Trevi Fountain just after sunset, turning water into liquid gold. Matt and I got properly lost in Trastevere on our third night, following the sound of laughter and clinking glasses down a narrow alley that opened onto a tiny piazza where an old man was serving the most divine gelato I've ever tasted.

We shared a single cup (because sharing is intimate, and also because we were both too full for two), our fingers brushing as we passed it back and forth. He has this habit of stealing the last bite when he thinks I'm not looking - a tiny betrayal I've come to adore. We sat on the edge of the fountain, feet dangling in the cool water, talking about everything and nothing until the gelato melted completely and we had to laugh at our sticky fingers.

— He still denies stealing my gelato. I have photographic evidence.

April 3, 2024

Paris in Spring: Why the Best Conversations Happen at 2 AM

They say Paris is for lovers, but nobody tells you that the real magic happens after midnight, when the crowds thin and the city reveals its quieter, more intimate self. Matt and I stumbled upon a tiny jazz club in Le Marais tucked behind an unmarked door, where the saxophone sounded like smoke and the whiskey was poured with a generous hand.

We didn't dance - we just sat in a corner booth, shoulders touching, watching the musician's fingers fly over the keys while we talked about everything from our first crushes to whether we believe in soulmates. He has this way of listening that makes you feel like you're the only person in the world, like your thoughts matter more than they possibly could. When we stumbled back to our Airbnb, laughing at our own bad French, he paused outside our door and kissed me properly - slow, deep, and utterly unhurried.

— I still dream about that saxophone solo

March 22, 2024

The London Flat Hunt: Or, How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Property Chain

Looking for a flat in London is less like house hunting and more like entering a gladiatorial arena where everyone's armed with spreadsheets and barely concealed desperation. After three weeks of viewing soul-crushing studios and apartments that claimed to be "cozy" (estate agent code for "you could touch both walls while sitting on the toilet"), Matt and I almost gave up - until we found it.

Top floor of a converted warehouse in Peckham, with exposed brick walls, windows that frame the city like a living painting, and a kitchen big enough for us to cook together without bumping into each other. The estate agent tried to upsell us on a parking space we don't need, but Matt just laughed and said we'd rather spend the money on takeaway curry and wine. We moved in last week, and every morning I wake up grateful for the way the light hits the brick wall at 7:15 AM.

— He insists on making tea every morning. It's becoming my favorite part of the day.

February 14, 2024

Valentine's Day: Or, Why We Celebrate Love by Burning Toast

We don't do fancy dinners or roses on Valentine's Day. Instead, we have our own tradition: attempting to cook a romantic meal together, which inevitably ends with one of us setting off the smoke alarm while the other tries desperately not to laugh. Last year it was the carbonara incident (too much garlic, not enough patience). This year, we attempted homemade pasta.

Let's just say that rolling out dough is harder than it looks, especially when you're distracted by the way your partner concentrates, tongue slightly poking out as they work. We ended up with lopsided tagliatelle, a sauce that was somehow both too thin and too thick, and garlic bread that qualified as a fire hazard. We ate it anyway, sitting on the floor of our kitchen because the table was covered in flour, and toasted with slightly burnt garlic bread and terrible wine.

— He says I'm hopeless in the kitchen. I say he's lucky I find it endearing.

January 10, 2024

New Year's Resolutions: Or, Why I Keep Mine and He Doesn't

I made three resolutions this year: to read more poetry, to say "no" to things that drain me, and to take one solo trip just because I can. Matt, meanwhile, resolved to "be less grumpy before coffee" and "try not to steal the duvet at night." We both know which of us is more likely to stick to their plans.

But here's the thing about resolutions - they're not really about the goals themselves. They're about the person you want to become while pursuing them. Mine are about carving out space for wonder and autonomy in a world that constantly asks for more. His are quieter, more domestic - small acts of consideration that add up to something profound when you notice them.

Last night, I woke up to find him had stolen exactly half the duvet, leaving me pleasantly chilly and utterly charmed by his commitment to self-improvement.

— I'm keeping the duvet theft as evidence for future negotiations

December 24, 2023

Christmas Eve: The Quiet Magic of Being Known

Christmas morning at our place is all about quiet comfort - scrambled eggs with too much black pepper, presents that are more thoughtful than expensive, and the specific way Matt looks at me when he thinks I'm not watching. There's a moment, usually around 10:30 AM when the wrapping paper is scattered everywhere and we're both slightly overwhelmed by the sheer *ness* of it all, when he reaches for my hand and squeezes it just once.

No words needed. Just that brief, solid pressure that says: I see you. I'm glad you're here. Let's do this again next year.

We don't do grand gestures or elaborate traditions. Our Christmas is built on the small things: the way he remembers how I take my tea, the fact that he still teases me about my obsession with true crime podcasts, and the comfortable silence we fall into after hours of being together.

— He got me a first edition of my favorite poetry collection. I'm still trying to figure out how to thank him properly.

November 5, 2023

Fireworks Night: Or, Why I Prefer Watching Matt Watch the Sky

Bonfire Night in London is a sensory overload in the best possible way - the smell of woodsmoke and toffee apples, the crackle of flames in the park, and the way the sky suddenly erupts in color and sound that makes you feel both insignificant and profoundly alive. We went to our local display with sparklers in hand and zero expectations, just wanting to be outside together.

I spent less time watching the fireworks and more time watching Matt's face as he watched them - the way his eyes widen at a particularly brilliant burst, the small smile that touches his lips when he's impressed, the soft exclamation that escapes him when something truly spectacular happens. There's something deeply intimate about being the person who gets to see someone else experience joy.

We ended the night sharing a thermos of spiced cider, our shoulders pressed together against the cold, already planning where we'll go next year.

— He claims he wasn't watching me watch him. The sparkler photo says otherwise.

November 3, 2024

The Changing Room Incident: A Vegetable-Induced Awkwardness

I never thought I’d witness Matt’s best friend standing stark naked in the gym changing rooms, holding a carrot and two Brussels sprouts like they were props in some bizarre surrealist sketch. We both froze—him, mortified; me, torn between laughter and the urge to look away. The vegetable combo was inexplicable, the situation even more so. After an eternity of strained smiles and muttered apologies, I slipped out, leaving them to recover their dignity.

— I still wonder what the carrot was for.

November 10, 2024

The Bedroom Audit: Age, Toys, and Honest Talk

Lately I’ve been wondering if the spark’s gone, if the ten‑year age gap is showing up in the bedroom as much as it does in our playlists. Matt’s energy is infectious, but I find myself reaching for the vibrator he gave me for my 52nd birthday more often than I used to. It’s not that I don’t want him—it’s that my body asks for a different rhythm now, and I’m learning to ask for it without shame.

— Maybe it’s not dryness, just a new frequency.
