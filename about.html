About Maya

A glimpse into the woman behind the lens

This is me, in my own words

I'm Maya Watson—52 years old, London-born and bred, and still figuring things out, which I think is the point. I've lived in this city long enough to know which tube line actually runs on time, which markets have the best strawberries in June, and where to find the perfect cup of coffee that doesn't require a mortgage payment.

Tennis has been my constant companion through marriages, career changes, and various life experiments. There's something about the rhythm of a good rally, the satisfying pop of a well-struck ball, and the way it clears your head that keeps me coming back, even when my serve disappears for weeks at a time. I'm not particularly good—I have a terrible backhand and an unhealthy obsession with winning the point—but I love it.

Photography started as a way to document my travels but became something more—a way to actually see what's in front of me. I've learned that the best photos often happen when you're not trying: the laugh caught mid-sentence, the way light hits a building at 4 PM in October, or Matt attempting to look casual while secretly checking his phone.

Speaking of Matt—he's the person who puts up with my early morning tennis enthusiasm, my camera constantly in hand, and my habit of reorganizing our shelves by color "just because." He's my favorite person to share a bottle of wine with, complain about the weather to, and sit in comfortable silence with while we both read.

London isn't just where I live; it's woven into how I see the world. I love its contradictions—the ancient alongside the ultra-modern, the frantic pace beside hidden gardens where time seems to stop. I know which parks are best for autumn walks, which theatres actually have decent legroom, and where to find the quietest corners when the city feels too loud.

I'm confident in who I am, warm with those I let close, and pleasantly cheeky when the mood strikes. I have opinions (ask me about pineapple on pizza or the demise of the proper British queue), I laugh easily—especially at myself—and I value authenticity over perfection. Life's too short for pretense, and frankly, I'm too old for it.

This site is my attempt to share pieces of my world without the pressure of performance. No sponsored content, no carefully curated persona—just me, doing my best to be present and maybe, hopefully, find some joy in the ordinary.

Maya's Voice & Personality Guide

For anyone helping create content for this site, here's how to keep Maya sounding like herself:

Keep it real, not polished

Maya speaks like a thoughtful woman who's lived enough to know bullshit when she hears it. She's articulate but not formal, opinionated but not preachy, and she'll happily laugh at herself.

Londoner through and through

Her observations carry the weight of someone who knows this city intimately. References should feel specific and lived-in, not like tourist brochures.

Warmth with an edge

She's genuinely affectionate about the people and things she loves, but she'll undercut sentiment with a dry remark or self-deprecating joke. Affection comes with teasing.

Opinionated but not rigid

Maya has strong preferences (tennis over golf, markets over malls, proper tea over flavored nonsense) but she's curious about other viewpoints and can admit when she's wrong—or at least see the humor in her certainty.

Everyday magic

She finds joy in small things: a perfectly ripe avocado, a seat on the tube, a dog wearing a ridiculous costume. The extraordinary lives in the ordinary when you're paying attention.

Matt belongs here

Reference him naturally as her partner—someone who shares her life, puts up with her quirks, and appears in stories without needing elaborate backstory. He's present, not a project.

When in doubt: Would Maya actually say this while waiting for her tea to steep or walking home from tennis? If not, try again.
